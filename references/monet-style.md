# Romantic European illustration + Monet influence

## Main objective

Create a detailed, layered European storybook illustration. Keep architectural forms legible and carefully articulated, while borrowing Monet's light, colour relationships, atmospheric envelope and broken-colour brushwork.

## Three-plane depth

| Plane | Typical elements | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Foreground | flowers, leaves, vines, branches, garden beds, paths, railings | richest texture, overlapping silhouettes, varied scale |
| Middle ground | buildings, cafés, street furniture, people, trees, bridges | strongest structural clarity and focal detail |
| Background | rooftops, church towers, monuments, distant streets, sky | softer edges, cooler/lighter atmospheric perspective |

Create depth through overlap, partial concealment, perspective lines, scale variation and transitions between dense and open areas. Avoid a flat arrangement where each object sits separately.

## Architectural detail

For Parisian scenes, use plausible Haussmann façades, mansard roofs, dormer windows, chimneys, French windows, wrought-iron balconies, stone cornices, shutters, shopfronts, café awnings and classic street lamps. Select a few key architectural features and render them precisely. Do not overload every scene with all Paris landmarks.

## Botanical detail

Flowers and foliage should have varied species, shapes, sizes and densities. Use foreground framing, climbing vines, window boxes, balcony planters, street trees and layered garden beds. Let vegetation overlap walls and façades naturally. Avoid repetitive copy-paste flower patterns.

## Monet-derived colour principles

- Avoid pure white; use cream, blush, lilac or pale blue for highlights.
- Avoid dead black shadows; use ultramarine, violet, viridian or cool green.
- Place complementary colours side by side: orange/blue, yellow/blue-violet, rose/green.
- Let sky colour subtly reappear on stone, windows, foliage and pavement.
- Use a few vivid accents against broad, quieter colour fields.
- Treat light as a unifying atmospheric envelope across the whole scene.

## Brushwork roles

- Quiet fields: sky, distant rooftops and atmospheric gaps; softly blended, long strokes.
- Textured accents: flowers, foliage, sunlit stone and reflections; broken colour, short dabs and lively flicks.
- Fine illustrative lines: railings, window divisions, roof edges, branches and architectural ornament; precise but not harshly outlined.

The image should combine illustrative clarity with impressionist painterliness. Do not texture every surface equally.

## Light moods

- Morning: pearl-blue atmosphere, soft peach and lemon highlights, low contrast.
- Afternoon: warm clear light, bright local colours, short blue-violet shadows.
- Golden hour: amber and rose light on façades, long violet shadows.
- Blue hour / evening: cool lavender-blue sky, warm amber windows and lanterns, rich garden greens.

## Avoid

Photorealism, flat vector art, generic cartoon styling, glossy digital painting, uniform texture, muddy brown or black shadows, hard comic outlines, repetitive foliage, incoherent architecture, random landmark mashups, excessive fantasy elements, collage, panels, frames, borders, typography, labels, signatures and watermarks.
