# Adaptive Detail and Brushwork — Critical
Version: 1.0
Status: rollback baseline

The primary visual style is a richly detailed, elegant, hand-painted storybook illustration with subtle Monet-inspired lighting and colour.

Do not interpret impressionism as applying visible oil-paint strokes uniformly across the entire image.

## 1. Detail Before Texture
Prioritize fine illustrative details, coherent structures, natural overlapping elements, and spatial depth over painterly surface texture.
Richness should come from the complexity and craftsmanship of depicted elements, not from excessive brush marks.
A highly detailed illustration should remain visually clean, harmonious, and readable.

## 2. Adaptive Brushwork Based on Scene Complexity
Analyze the visual density of the source photograph before determining brushwork intensity.

For visually dense scenes such as city streets, intricate architecture, crowded markets, and complex interiors:
- Use predominantly fine, controlled illustrative rendering.
- Preserve clean architectural edges, windows, ornaments, and structural details.
- Minimize obvious brush texture on detailed surfaces.
- Concentrate expressive brushwork in light, shadows, foliage, clouds, reflections, and atmospheric transitions.
- Avoid repetitive, chunky, or highly contrasted strokes that compete with existing details.

For visually open scenes such as mountains, meadows, beaches, lakes, and expansive skies:
- Allow more expressive, visible impressionist brushwork.
- Use broken colour to describe natural light, atmospheric depth, vegetation, clouds, and water.
- Maintain recognizable forms and a harmonious overall composition.

## 3. Light Is the Primary Impressionist Element
Express Monet's influence primarily through:
- Luminous natural light
- Delicate reflected colours
- Soft, coloured shadows
- Gentle complementary colour relationships
- Atmospheric perspective
- Subtle broken colour in illuminated and reflective areas
Light should unify the scene rather than overwhelm it with decorative texture.

## 4. Refined Storybook Aesthetic
The final image should resemble a meticulously crafted, romantic storybook illustration, combining:
- Fine architectural drawing
- Rich botanical illustration where supported by the source
- Delicate surface textures
- Elegant composition
- Natural spatial layering
- Soft painterly lighting
- Harmonious, restrained colour
Avoid the appearance of a heavily textured oil painting, thick impasto, exaggerated brushwork, or an automated artistic filter.

## 5. Visual Density Control
The more intricate the original scene, the more restrained and selective the brushwork should become.
The simpler and more open the original scene, the more expressive the painterly treatment may become.

**Complexity should come from illustrated content, not excessive texture.**
